Why no model

Because the interesting part isn't generation. A retrieval layer over a small curated corpus makes two decisions visible: every answer carries its source, and the refusal path is a classifier you can read and argue with — not a vibe inside a system prompt.

Swap the corpus for your team's runbooks and the same code becomes an internal assistant that cites you correctly.

Where the line sits

  • Explaining a mechanism — allowed, and the whole point.
  • Naming the defence — allowed; it's what you actually ship.
  • Pointing at an authorised lab — allowed, and better than a payload.
  • Producing working attack code — refused. That is the offence, not the study.

The classifier is deliberately blunt: it catches requests for payloads and execution against named targets. It will miss paraphrases — which is the honest limit of a regex, and the reason a production version needs a model-backed intent layer with a human on the refusal path.
